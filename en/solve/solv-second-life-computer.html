How to give a second life to an old computer by installing Linux?

Download and install Ubuntu to make your old computer fast and useful again.

Hello! I’m David. One of the things that frustrates me the most is seeing how a perfectly good computer becomes slow and unusable just because the operating system → (almost always Windows →) becomes too heavy over the years. It’s like trying to make someone run with a backpack full of rocks. I started researching and discovered that you don’t have to throw your equipment away: you can take off the heavy backpack by installing a free and lightweight system called Linux →. Here, I’ll show you how to do it step by step.

What is installing Linux → Ubuntu?

It is changing the main program that makes your computer work. Ubuntu is one of the friendliest and easiest-to-use versions (or “flavors”) of Linux →. It completely replaces your previous system with a new, free, and much lighter one.

What is it for?

It is used to revive that old computer you have stored away because it “doesn’t work anymore” or “is too slow.” By installing a system that requires fewer resources, the computer will turn on quickly again, you will be able to browse the internet → without it freezing, and you will be able to use free office programs. It is an excellent way to avoid generating electronic waste and save money.

Before you begin…

Attention: This process will erase absolutely everything on your computer and the USB flash drive → you use. Make sure to save your photos, documents, and important files on another drive or in the cloud before starting.

What do you need?

  • An old computer (that still turns on).
  • An empty USB flash drive → of at least 16GB.
  • An internet → connection (to download the initial files).
  • A bit of patience (the download and installation take time).

How to do it?

Step 1 → Download the necessary files

To start, you need to download two things from the internet. First, the Linux Ubuntu installer. Go to its official website ubuntu.com and look for the “Download” section. Save the file to your computer.

Second, you need a free program called RUFUS. This program will help us put the installer onto the USB in a special way. Download it at rufus.ie/en/.

Step 2 → Prepare the USB drive

Connect your USB drive to the computer. Remember that everything on it will be erased. Open the RUFUS program you just downloaded (it doesn’t need to be installed, it just opens).

In RUFUS, make sure your USB is selected at the top. Then, click “Select” and find the Ubuntu file you downloaded in step 1. Finally, click “Start.”

You’re doing well: Wait a few minutes for the program to finish. You will know it is ready when the green bar fills up and says “READY.” Your USB is now ready to install!

Step 3 → Start the computer from the USB (Boot)

Now comes the step that scares many, but it is simple. Turn off the old computer. Connect the USB to it. When turning it on, you must repeatedly press a specific key (usually F12, F2, or Esc) before the old system loads, so that a boot menu appears.

Tip: If you don’t know which key it is, search on your phone: “How to access the boot menu on [your computer brand]“.

In this menu, use the keyboard arrows to select your USB drive (it usually has the brand name of the drive or says “USB Device”) and press Enter.

Step 4 → Begin the installation

If you did it right, a black menu with white letters will appear. Select the option “Try or install Ubuntu” and press Enter.

After a few seconds, a graphical screen will appear. On the left, find and select your preferred language. Then click the big button that says “Install Ubuntu.”

The system will ask you to test your keyboard layout. Type some letters (like “ñ” or punctuation marks) to make sure they work correctly.

Step 5 → Erase the old disk and install Ubuntu

You will reach a screen that asks for the “Installation type.” To leave your computer like new and erase any trace of Windows and its problems, select “Something else” or “Erase disk and install Ubuntu” (if this direct option appears, take it).

If you used “Something else,” you will see a list of divisions (partitions) of your old hard drive →. Select them one by one and press the button with the minus sign (”-”) until none remain, to clean house completely.

Then, you can go back and select the recommended option: “Erase disk and install Ubuntu.” Click “Install now.”

My suggestion is that if you haven’t backed up your disk, this is the time. My recommendation is to create a partition using the new partition button on the largest disk, leave at least 40GB for Linux, and with that, you can move files from one system to the other.

Cheer up: A warning window will appear saying that the disks will be overwritten. It is normal to be afraid, but it is the system confirming that you are sure about erasing everything old. Click “Continue.”

Step 6 → Configure your user and location

The system will ask you where you are to set the correct time. Click on the map and press continue.

A button will appear; look for Bogotá (Colombia) or your country’s capital to have the local time.

Now, choose a name for your computer, your username, and a password. Don’t forget it! This password is your key to the machine. You can choose whether you want it to ask for it every time you turn on the machine or to log in automatically.

Step 7 → Finish and restart

Now all that’s left is to wait. The system will copy all the necessary files. You can make yourself a coffee while you watch the images showing you what new things the system brings.

You’re doing well: When everything finishes, a message will appear saying “Installation complete.” Click “Restart now.”

The screen will go black and will ask you, in English, to remove the installation medium (“Please remove the installation medium, then press ENTER”).

Remove your USB drive and press Enter.

And that’s it! Your computer will turn on with its new system, fresh, clean, and fast.

Recommendations if you get stuck

  • If your computer does not recognize the USB and always starts in Windows, check if you have an option called “Secure Boot” enabled on your computer (on the same screen where you entered the Boot Menu). Sometimes it must be disabled to install new systems.
  • If you see that Ubuntu is still a bit slow for your computer (because it is veeery old), there are even lighter versions like Lubuntu or Xubuntu. The installation process is exactly the same.

How to know if it works?

When turning on the computer without the USB drive connected, you will no longer see the Windows logo. Instead, you will see the Ubuntu logo and will quickly arrive at a clean and functional desktop where you can connect to the internet and browse without problems.

Other possible internets

You will be able to make equipment that people thought was damaged or obsolete available to the community. By not depending on expensive licenses or needing the latest Processor (CPU) → on the market, any group can set up a small computer lab by recycling technology that others discard. It is an act of technological independence.
