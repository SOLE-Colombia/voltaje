How to generate electricity using a bicycle?

Get some juice flowing by building a bike-generator.

Hi, I’m Nicolás. I’m what you might call a “tinkerer” or a cacharrero. I’m an engineer, and I’m fascinated by how things work. I enjoy taking gadgets apart and putting them back together just to see what makes them tick, and I also love figuring out how we can make the most of the resources we have in our community.

What is a bike generator?

A bike generator is a system that converts the mechanical energy from a bicycle into electrical energy. Basically, it turns your pedaling motion into electricity, which lets you power all sorts of things, like charging your electronics or even running lighting systems. (Source: Ecologismos)

What is it for?

This solution is a step-by-step guide on how to buy and assemble a power system using a bicycle. We’ve calculated it so that a laptop and a router can run for 3 hours, which is about how long a SOLE session usually lasts.

What do you need?

  • Money to buy batteries, cables, a power inverter, and a charge controller.
  • Energy to pedal.
  • An old bicycle.
  • Patience and plenty of enthusiasm to put the system together.
  • A stand to keep the bike stationary.

How to do it?

Step 1 → Calculate your energy needs

Figure out how much power you actually need. In this case, we need to power a laptop and a router for 3 hours. This will determine the capacity your system needs and how long you’ll have to pedal to generate the juice.

  • A laptop consumes 120W/h
  • A router consumes 15W/h

Now, let’s calculate the total energy load. You can find this info on the back of your devices or on their power bricks. For a SOLE session, which lasts 3 hours, for example:

  • Laptop: 120W/h x 3 hours = 360W
  • router: 15W/h x 3 hours = 45W

Adding those up gives us 405W over 3 hours. If we divide 405W / 3h, we get 135 W/h.

The bike machine can generate up to 200 watts/hour at a moderate pedaling speed, and up to 400 watts/hour at a high speed.

Step 2 → Estimate the equipment specs

Remember, to build any power generation system, you’ll need these components:

An electricity generator This is any device capable of capturing and transforming some form of energy (mechanical, solar, thermal, hydraulic, chemical) into electrical energy. In this case, it’s the bicycle combined with an electric motor and a stationary stand.

A charge controller Its job is to regulate the flow of energy from the motor to the batteries, controlling both the current and the voltage.

A battery It stores the generated energy as chemical energy and releases it later as controlled direct current (DC).

A power inverter This converts the direct current (DC) coming out of the battery into alternating current (AC) so we can plug in our devices.

Solar cable and accessories You need electrical cable that’s sturdy enough to carry the electricity we’re generating.

Usually, solar cables are sized using the AWG (American Wire Gauge) standard.

Look for an electrical supply shop and get quotes for this equipment. Compare costs between different suppliers and buy based on trust, warranty, and price.

How to use it?

Precautions

Heads up: Only connect one pole at a time so you don’t get zapped. It’s a good idea to work with rubber gloves.

Connecting the circuit The motor has a negative and a positive pole. Connect the cables coming from the motor to the controller, and from there to the battery.

How do you know if this solution works?

With this setup, you’ll have enough electricity to power your SOLE. If you can turn on your computer and router just by pedaling, it works!
