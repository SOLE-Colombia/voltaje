How to get donated equipment?

Giving a second use to equipment that would otherwise be discarded.

Hello, are you there? My name is Mateo and I’ll be joining you in developing this solution. If you are reading, listening to, or watching this, it’s because you’re interested or curious about how to get donated equipment. If that’s the case, you’re in the right place.

What is getting donated equipment?

In a nutshell, this solution is about reuse. It’s about being consistent with caring for the world we live in; it’s about taking advantage of things that are obsolete to others, but that we might be able to use for a long time to come.

This solution is for giving a second chance to equipment that is no longer needed. Did you know there are companies that want to upgrade their gear and donate what they have? This equipment is usually in good condition and your community could put it to good use.

What is this solution for?

While this solution doesn’t require money to purchase equipment, keep in mind that it can take weeks, or even months, depending on the steps we’ll share below. You have a better chance of receiving a donation if you can demonstrate that the equipment will be used for community purposes and that its goal is to improve people’s quality of life.

What do you need?

  • Persistence.
  • Patience.
  • Support from others. Teamwork increases your chances of success.

How to do it?

Let’s get started!

This solution is developed in three steps where we’ll share everything we know and have learned at Fundación SOLE Colombia about how to get donated equipment. Every case is different, so it’s important that you’re willing to adapt these steps depending on the hurdles you find along the way.

Photo: veerasak Piyawatanakul - Pexels


Step 1 → Find the donor company and identify the relationship model

Generally, equipment donors are companies with significant financial capacity, payroll, and infrastructure. Think of telecommunications or computer companies, universities, or look into sponsors of social projects in your region. That said, we suggest starting with smaller companies. You have two options for this:

  • Option 1: Direct search

You can look for companies or individuals who donate equipment via the internet, social media, or by checking with your network of contacts and close circles.

💬 For option 1, make a list of companies and the ways you can contact them—it could be through the people who handle the organization’s finances or those who manage their computer equipment.

The key is to get a direct contact at the donor company with whom you can speak in person, send an email, make a call, or send a letter. If you send a letter or email, make sure it will be read, and if the person isn’t the one making the decision, ensure they will redirect it to the right person.

  • Option 2: Outreach

You can write a post or announcement on social media making it clear that: 1. you need donated equipment in good condition; 2. you have a community project that will benefit many people; 3. include a number where they can reach you; 4. be clear about who you are and try to make the message evoke empathy and trust. An example message could be:

“Hi everyone! My name is Amaranta and I’m part of the XYZ FOUNDATION. Does anyone know of organizations that are donating computer equipment in good condition? We are looking for support to implement a project with seniors in rural areas of Colombia. Any information or contact would be a huge help. Thanks!

My contact number is 123456789

We suggest doing both options at the same time; this will broaden your chances of finding a donor company.

💪🏽 Keep it up! At this point, it’s key to be persistent. There’s no magic formula; it’s a matter of knocking on doors and paving the way. In any case, if you’ve read this far, it’s because you have the drive to do it, and that is something to admire.


Step 2 → Wait for a response and don’t let the contact “go cold.”

Photo by Fauxels: Pexels

Once you’ve sent your requests, all that’s left is to wait and follow up continuously to get a response. Don’t let the contact go cold; if more than a week passes without a reply, write again or go in person if possible. When you get an affirmative response, be clear about the model under which you will receive the equipment: not all companies donate their equipment; some decide to give it away as a gift. There are two types of models:

  • Option 1: Gifting equipment

You might find a company or person who is simply giving their equipment away. Family members might give away equipment. When you contact them, make sure you understand the terms under which you will receive the equipment in this scenario.

  • Option 2: Donating equipment

Now, for companies that donate, it’s a different model where a second chance is given to equipment that is in good condition and can be used by people and communities who need it.

How does it work? Many companies have to perform upgrades that require changing their equipment or they need new technology to carry out their work, so they decide to donate and can receive a tax deduction.

⚠️ Keep in mind that for this to be possible, donor companies need to receive a donation certificate. If you aren’t part of a foundation, you must find an ally who can issue this certificate for you.

Who issues this certificate and how for companies that want to donate? To find an ally, think of a non-profit entity that has an active legal status and can issue these certificates for the companies donating the equipment. For example, foundations, cooperatives, or similar entities in your region. Talk to them and tell them about your purpose. If they decide to support you, ask them for guidance in this process.

We invite you to look up the story of Alex Cortés, an engineer who founded Fundación Click → with the goal of giving a second life to computers that many people, organizations, or companies no longer need; he decides to repair them, perform maintenance, and give them away or donate them to those who need them most. If you want to know more about his story, we invite you to look him up, along with his foundation and his journey.

⚠️⚠️ Important: You must be clear about how the equipment will be used, as legally the equipment will belong to the ally you found. Don’t forget to check that the equipment they give you is functional. We don’t want you to end up storing electronic waste that you’ll have to repair.


Step 3 → Receiving the equipment and building the relationship

Photo: Ketut Subiyanto - Pexels

Photo: Cytonn photography - Pexels

For the receipt of the equipment, in either of the two models, keep in mind that every company or person has their own procedure.

The delivery of the equipment might be a shipment, or you might have to pick it up or reach an agreement on the delivery location with the company or person making the donation. After delivery, it’s important to know what kind of relationship you want and can build with them:

  • Option 1: Gifting equipment

In this model, paperwork is usually not required to formalize the delivery. However, it is likely that the donors will want to establish a short- or medium-term relationship to know the destination and use of the equipment. If this is your case, we suggest making it explicit and maintaining cordial communication with them.

For example, although it is not mandatory, at Fundación SOLE Colombia, we usually formalize the process through a thank-you letter signed by both parties, where the delivery, the condition of the equipment, and our commitment to put it to good use are corroborated.


  • Option 2: Donating equipment

For the delivery, make a donation request explaining your interests, conditions, and the cooperation you will build.

How many computers do you need? What condition is the equipment you are going to receive in? What does the donor company need from you? What will you use the equipment for? Who will be the people and communities benefiting? For this option, we suggest that, just like in option 1, you consider the possibility of building a bond with the donor company.

At SOLE Colombia, we usually send the donor companies photographs that thank them and show the community use of the equipment, with which we try to open the door to future donations.


What does community use of equipment imply?

Do you remember what we mentioned at the beginning of this solution about the chances you have of receiving a donation if you demonstrate that the use of the equipment will be communal? Now we invite you to reflect on the importance that collective use of equipment can have in a community. It’s not just about receiving them, but about reaching agreements on who will manage them, how they will be used, and how often; this will involve fostering dialogue and teamwork. It’s not about having a lot of equipment, but about knowing how to use it as a community.

How to know if this solution works?

When you receive equipment that others aren’t using, you’ve proven that this solution works. You will also have created a relationship with someone; that is beautiful and powerful. Someone who has tuned into your purpose and wants to lend you a hand.

Other possible Internets

When you cultivate the relationship with your donors, it’s possible they might make donations more than once. Can you imagine if they donated a few computers to you every year? What would you do then with all that equipment?
