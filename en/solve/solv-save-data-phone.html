¿Cómo hacer que los datos de tu celular duren más?

Trucos prácticos y reflexiones para administrar tu plan y que el internet te alcance para lo importante.

Hi! I’m Catalina. Has it ever happened to you that you get a message saying your data has run out and you don’t even know where it went? It used to happen to me all the time and it was very frustrating, especially when I needed the internet for something really important like homework or an errand.

I’m not a systems expert, but I started “tinkering” with my phone’s settings and discovered that there are several open faucets where our internet leaks out. I’ve learned that with a couple of methodical adjustments and, above all, by being more conscious of how we use our cell phones, we can make the same plan last much longer. Here, I’ll show you how I did it!

What is data management?

It is learning how to configure your phone so that it only spends internet on what you decide. But before moving any buttons, it is important to ask ourselves: What do I want to spend my data on today? Many times we lose it watching videos that don’t even interest us, just out of inertia. Managing is taking control of your connection.

What is it for and what can you achieve?

This solution allows you to have real control over your spending and ensures that you don’t get left disconnected in the middle of the month. In the end, this can save you money and gives you the freedom to use your internet for what is truly valuable to you and your community.

Before you start…

For these tricks to work, it is best to do them while you are connected to a WiFi network (in a park or at a neighbor’s house), so you don’t spend your own data while configuring the savings.

Question to think about: Before looking for a more expensive plan, have you tried closing the “faucets” where your current data is leaking? Sometimes, what we need is not more internet, but better-used internet.

What do you need?

  1. Your cell phone with an active data plan.
  2. A few calm minutes to check the settings.
  3. A “tinkerer’s” spirit! Don’t be afraid to explore your phone’s menus.

How to do it?

Step 1 → Identify who is “eating” your data

Go to Settings and look for the “Data usage” or “Mobile network” option. There you will see a list of the applications that have used the most internet in the last month.


Step 2 → Set a limit so you don’t get any surprises

In the same data menu, look for “Data limit” or “Data warning”. Configure it so that the phone notifies you when you reach a daily amount (for example, 600 MB). It’s a way of telling yourself: “Watch out! You’ve already spent today’s allowance.”


Step 3 → Activate savings “app by app”

Enter the applications you use the most (such as Instagram, Facebook, or YouTube). Look in their own settings for the “Data saver” or “Content quality” option. Activate it so that videos do NOT play automatically. This way, you only spend internet on the video you really want to watch.


Recommendations

  • Do I really need it now? Before opening a heavy application, ask yourself if it can wait until you are in an area with free WiFi.
  • Clean up your phone: Disable “Auto-update” for applications in the Play Store; that consumes entire gigabytes without you even noticing.
  • The airplane mode trick: If you are not going to use your phone for a long time, turn off mobile data. You prevent the phone from spending internet “searching” for a signal or background updates.

Other possible internets

Managing our individual data well is the first step toward thinking about a collective internet. When we stop being passive consumers of what social networks push on us, we start using technology for what is truly useful to us.

Imagine if what we saved individually could be pooled to pay for a community connection that belongs to everyone. Other internets are possible when we remember that the internet is a tool, not an end in itself. By taking care of your data, you are taking care of a resource that allows you to stay connected with what you love.
