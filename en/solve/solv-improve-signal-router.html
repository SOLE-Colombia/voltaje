How to boost your modem signal?

Some tips to get the most out of your router.

Hi! I’m Manu, the storyteller for SOLE Colombia, and I’ve also felt that frustration of losing my Internet connection right in the middle of an important meeting or a SOLE session.

What does improving your router signal mean?

Just when you need it most, the connection disappears—has that happened to you? It has to me, more than once. But don’t worry, because you’re about to learn how to improve the signal of your Router so that happens less often.

I’ll also show you how to tweak your Router settings to browse faster by changing the servers for DNS and adjusting your WiFi channel. This isn’t hard, and you can do it right from home! So get ready, we’re going to get the most out of your network.

What is this for?

I’m going to walk you through getting the most out of your Router. Together, we’ll improve how the WiFi signal is distributed in your space, achieving a more stable connection so it doesn’t fail you when you need it most—whether it’s for a meeting, a class, or a SOLE session. No more interrupted signals when you’re investigating a Big Question!

Remember, these tweaks won’t increase the speed you’re paying for, but they will help the signal reach every corner of your house better by optimizing your DNS.

What do you need?

  • To use an Internet service that connects through a Router.
  • The login credentials for your Router.
  • A computer connected to the network.
  • A bit of patience (hopefully not too much!).
  • A tinkerer’s attitude.

How to do it?

Alright, I’m going to guide you step-by-step to improve your WiFi signal. Trust me! Something is bound to change!

Step 1 → Measure your connection speed

The first thing you need to do is measure your current connection speed. In this solution, we tell you how to do it: ¿Cómo saber la velocidad de tu conexión a Internet?

Knowing your speed will let you compare the “before” and “after” once you’ve made the adjustments.

Step 2 → Explore your space and locate the Router

It might seem obvious, but we often forget the simplest thing: get closer to the Router. If you’re organizing a SOLE session or have several devices connected, make sure they are as close as possible so the signal hits harder.

Where you put the Router is key. Make sure to place it in an elevated spot, like on a table or even mounted on the wall. Ideally, it should be in a central location, not in a corner or near a window, which is where installers often leave them. Moving the Router to a more strategic spot will help the signal spread more evenly throughout the house.

Step 3 → Position the Router antennas perpendicularly

Have you looked at your router’s antennas? Check them right now. An important detail to improve the signal is to make sure they are in a perpendicular position—that is, pointing toward the ceiling. This helps the signal waves distribute better.

Make that adjustment and you’ll notice the difference. Well done!

With these 3 actions, you should already see some improvement. If you want to go further, check out the more advanced steps below.

Step 4 → Configure the DNS

What the heck is that?

DNS is like an address book that helps your computer find websites faster. By using faster DNS servers, like Google’s or Cloudflare’s, you ensure that pages load with more speed. The default servers from your Internet provider aren’t always the most optimal, so switching to more efficient ones can improve your browsing experience.

Now we’re going to do something interesting: configure the DNS. This will help the web pages you visit load faster, since DNS are the directories of IP addresses that the Router consults when browsing.

If you want to switch to faster and more reliable DNS servers, it will make your Internet queries snappier. To do this, follow these steps:

  1. Access your Router’s admin interface. To do this, in your computer’s network settings, look for the gateway. There you will find an IP address, something like 192.168.0.1 or 192.168.1.1.
  2. Log in with the credentials, which are usually admin/admin, or check your device’s manual for the default passwords.
  3. Change the values to 1.1.1.1, 1.0.0.1 (from Cloudflare) and 8.8.8.8 (from Google).
  4. Open your browser and enter that address in the address bar. This will take you to the Router control panel.
  5. Look for the Network Settings section, and there, the DNS Servers option.
  6. Save the changes and restart the Router.

Very good! Now your network will use these DNS servers, which are faster and more reliable.

Step 5 → Configure the WiFi channel

Another key adjustment is the WiFi channel configuration. Channels determine the frequency at which the Router broadcasts the signal. To avoid interference with other nearby networks, set your Router to the recommended channels: 1, 6, or 11. To do this:

  1. Enter your Router’s configuration interface again.
  2. Look for the Wireless section.
  3. Choose one of the recommended channels: 1, 6, or 11.
  4. Save the changes and restart the Router.

With this adjustment, you’ll reduce interference and improve signal quality. Great job, we’re almost done!

How do I know if this solution works?

Did you manage it? I’m sure you did! By following these steps, I’m sure your Router signal will have improved, and therefore, the distribution of WiFi in your space.

Other possible Internets

Although you won’t be able to increase the speed beyond what you’re paying for :c, you will have ensured that every corner of your house receives a stronger and more stable signal.
