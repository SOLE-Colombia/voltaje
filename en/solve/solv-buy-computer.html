How to buy a computer?

The best computer that fits our capacity and needs.

Hi, I’m Nicolás, and just like you, I’ve been learning how to improve my internet connection. I’m no expert, but by digging around online, watching tutorials, and learning through trial and error, I’ve managed to boost internet quality in areas with poor connectivity.

What does buying a computer look like for you?

In this guide, I’ll help you clear up some important points to help you make the right call. We’ll break down the main features of a computer and look at some recommendations based on those specs. You’ll also be able to weigh your own needs and budget. I hope you feel supported and confident as you go through the process of picking out a machine.

What is this for?

Making a purchase in a field where we aren’t experts can be a real headache. That’s why we put this solution together—to walk you through the process. At the end of the day, this guide is here to help you make an informed decision so you don’t waste your hard-earned money or end up with a computer that doesn’t actually do what you need it to.

How do I know if I need one?

Deciding whether or not to make a purchase comes down to two fundamental questions:

  • Think it over carefully: What need is this computer actually solving? Can this need be met in another way? For example, can you do the same thing with the phone you already have, or maybe a tablet?
  • If you’ve confirmed that the need can only be met with a computer, it’s time to look at other options before buying. For instance, can you borrow a computer from school or the library? Can you get a donated machine from a family member or a local business?

If you’ve exhausted those options and have definitely decided to buy a computer, then this guide is for you.

What are the advantages of a computer over a phone?

Speed and capacity are two of the biggest perks computers have over phones. With a computer, you’ve got a keyboard and a mouse at your disposal, which lets you type and navigate menus way faster. Plus, a computer’s larger screen lets you organize your digital workspace in a way a tiny phone screen just can’t. It also allows you to…

These factors don’t just save you time; they also let you multitask. On a computer, you can switch between apps more easily and even have them open side-by-side on the screen. For example, you can have a window open with your resume and another with a job you’re applying for. Or, if you’re hunting for the best deal, you can have pages for several products open at once to compare them easily.

What are the options?

  • Desktop Computer

This is a computer designed to stay in one spot, like a desk or table. The hardware components are usually housed in a “tower” or case that holds the motherboard and all the other parts.

  • Laptop

Often just called a “laptop,” this is a computer you can move or carry around with relative ease. Laptops are small, light, and specifically designed so their batteries can keep them running for a good chunk of time (usually 4 to 5 hours). Screen sizes typically range from 11 to 15 inches.

What should I keep in mind to make this decision?

Step 0 → Before we start: ALERT! What are hardware and software?

It’s important to know there are two basic parts to a computer: Hardware → and software:

  • Hardware →: The physical parts that make up the computer.
  • Software: The programs, instructions, data, and rules that tell the computer how to perform specific tasks.

Below, we’ll explain the main hardware components that matter when you’re buying.

ElementImageDescription
Processor (CPU) →This is the brain of the machine—definitely one of the most important factors. Remember, the more powerful this is, the faster your computer will run your commands.
StorageThe hard drive → determines how much stuff you can save on your machine.
RAM MemoryThe RAM memory → temporarily stores data for the apps you’re currently using. The more RAM you have, the better your computer handles running multiple programs at once.
Graphics CardThe graphics card handles processing data related to the images and videos you see on your screen.
PeripheralsThese are the input and output ports for other devices you’ll plug into your computer. For example: USB ports →, audio jacks, HDMI, etc.
ScreenThis is what lets you see what you’re doing.
BatteryOnly applies to laptops; it determines how long you can use it without being plugged into the wall.

Step 2 → Let’s make the decision

Now that you know the basics, it’s time to decide. We’ve put together some simple questions; depending on your answers, we’d recommend one hardware spec over another:

  • Criterion 0: Set a budget

Time to define your budget! You can calculate how many days of work it takes to earn the cost of the computer. Ultimately, the decision will come down to this. This purchase can easily represent more than a month’s work, so it’s very important to be as informed as possible before you pull the trigger.

  • Criterion 1: Usage habits

Let’s look at the most important questions for defining your habits:

  • Do I need the computer to be portable, or will I use it in one place?
  • Will I use it for gaming and design, or just for office work?
  • How much information am I going to save?
  • Do I need to run several programs at the same time?

With these questions and your budget, you can narrow down your list of options. Choose wisely.

Don’t get fooled

It’s important to remember that when you go to a store, a salesperson usually tries to push the “star product.” However, computers from 6 months or a year ago are often significantly cheaper than the latest tech, while still being great. Sometimes, you can find a used computer that’s been well-cared for and get a great deal. Also, I’d recommend buying a desktop or a laptop that allows you to upgrade its hardware if possible.

The computer I’m writing this on, I bought 13 years ago. It’s had a hard drive swap and a RAM upgrade, plus a few repairs, but it still works great and has been a fantastic long-term investment.

Finally, remember that it’s very easy to find pages and people dedicated to writing reviews and evaluating computer performance. Some sites even let you compare options. Once you’ve picked out the computers on the market that fit your needs, use these sites and user comments to make a more accurate decision.

One last tip that never hurts: go by the reputation of the brand and the seller. From my experience, I recommend ASUS for value for money, though at SOLE, we’ve also had good luck with Lenovo.

How much is enough?

This is a tough one, but I think we can rephrase it: Does the option you chose meet the need and intention you started with? In my case, 13 years ago, I bought a computer for university, but I also wanted to play games and download/watch lots of movies. If I had gone with the bare minimum (just for university), I should have picked a lightweight laptop with good battery life, a small screen, a mid-range processor, 8GB of RAM, and a basic graphics card. Instead, I did the exact opposite: I bought a big, heavy computer with poor battery life and a dedicated graphics card—what you’d call a “gamer” laptop: the ASUS N53SV.

Because I didn’t think it through, I ended up with a computer that was too big and heavy to carry around, with a battery that died in no time. In the end, its main job was university work; the gaming and movies were secondary. Now, even though I still use it, I almost never move it. It basically became a desktop. That’s why, here at SOLE Voltaje, we don’t want you to make the same mistakes I did! Always remember that often, “less is more.”

With what we’ve covered, nobody can pull the wool over your eyes now—it’s time to make the call!
