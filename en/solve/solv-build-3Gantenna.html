Build a DIY 3G antenna

Boost your signal with a homemade antenna.

Hi, I’m Nicolás. For a while now, I’ve been learning how to improve my connectivity. I’m no expert, but through internet searches, tutorials, and, above all, trial and error, I’ve learned a ton.

I’ve already tested and installed several solutions, and today, I’m sharing one of them with you: the DIY 3G antenna. Let’s get started!

What is building a DIY 3G antenna?

Building a 3G antenna means that by gathering and assembling easy-to-find materials, you can boost the strength of the 3G and 4G signal for a cell phone or a MiFi-type modem.

What is it for?

This DIY antenna will increase the ability to receive the waves that carry the 4G signal (mobile internet), improving the connection quality for your cell phone or shared modem (MiFi).

What do you need?

  1. 3mm thick aluminum rod (it has to be exactly 3mm).
  2. 50 cm of plastic cable trunking (ideally 2cm wide).
  3. Quick-drying epoxy glue (usually comes in two tubes).
  4. 10 meters of coaxial cable (or whatever length you need for the antenna to reach the connection point).
  5. 2 clamps or vises.
  6. 2 locking pliers (vise-grips).
  7. 20 cm of copper wire (recommended: 1mm thick).
  8. A ruler (even better if you have a caliper; it lets you be more precise).
  9. Hacksaw (if you can get an electric grinder with a thin cutting disc, you’ll save a looot of time).
  10. Fine-tip permanent marker (e.g., Sharpie).
  11. Pencil.
  12. Soldering iron, solder (tin), soldering paste (flux).
  13. Pliers and wire cutters (the latter is optional).
  14. Metal file.
  15. Workbench with good lighting.
  16. Plastic zip ties (also known as “cable ties”).
  17. Pole to mount the antenna (a bamboo pole as long as possible or a rigid rod of any material).
  18. Utility knife, cutter.
  19. Wire stripper (optional).
  20. Cell phone or shared modem (MiFi).
  21. Plus… good vibes.
  22. Patience.
  23. Lots of precision.

How to do it?

Step 0 → Follow these safety tips

This solution has an intermediate difficulty level and involves using some tools that can cause accidents if you aren’t careful. That’s why it’s important to keep the following recommendations in mind:

  • When using any sharp object, position it so that the cutting force is directed away from your body, not toward it.
  • Keep pets or children away from the workspace, mainly because of the risk of contact with a hot soldering iron.
  • Before cutting, make sure the clamps, vises, or pliers holding the material don’t allow it to move even slightly when you apply force.
  • When using solder and epoxy glue, protect your work surface so you don’t damage it.
  • Do not hoist the antenna during storms, as the antenna can act as a lightning rod and attract a strike to your surroundings.

Step 1 → Check your materials

Before starting, check and have all the necessary items on hand. If you don’t recognize any of the materials, you can find them in the photo where the materials are numbered.

You can buy these at your nearest hardware store since they are common materials; it shouldn’t be too hard to find them. Perhaps the most difficult (and important) item on the list is the aluminum rod. Sometimes you can find parts made with this type of rod in the metal racks sold in stores that carry lots of imported goods. At SOLE Voltaje, we’ve used different metals: copper, welding rods, and galvanized wire. We chose to buy a 3mm steel welding rod. If you can’t find aluminum at the hardware store, you can ask for other options; the important thing is that its diameter is as close as possible to 3mm or 1/8 of an inch.

Now, organize your workspace, keep these instructions handy, and divide the tasks among the people helping: someone can be in charge of measuring, others of cutting, organizing, and gluing.


Step 2 → Understand the diagram

Below, we show you the final diagram and some parts of the assembly so you have a clearer idea of the final setup.

Step 3 → Measure, cut, and mark

It’s time to start! Let’s get to work. You need to cut each of the 10 elements that make up the antenna. With the help of the caliper (or ruler), you have to mark the length of each one and cut them with the hacksaw or grinder. It’s recommended to cut a millimeter or half-millimeter extra and finish adjusting the length by filing the edges with the metal file. Listed below are the lengths of the 10 metal rods you need to cut; in the world of antennas, these have specific names (director, reflector, and dipole) related to the function they perform.

This is the list of cuts that are necessary:

  1. Reflector: 8.0 cm
  2. Dipole: 18 cm (This will be the length of the element; the bent rod should be exactly 7.6 cm, for now, just cut the rod to this length).
  3. Director #1: 7.6 cm
  4. Director #2: 7.5 cm
  5. Director #3: 7.4 cm
  6. Director #4: 7.4 cm
  7. Director #5: 7.3 cm
  8. Director #6: 7.3 cm
  9. Director #7: 7.2 cm
  10. Director #8: 7.2 cm

📏 Remember that 10 mm = 1 cm

It’s important to mark each of the cuts with tape, as seen in the photo, to make the next step easier.


Step 4 → Bend the dipole

The dipole is an element bent into a “C” shape. Its total length should be 7.6 cm, but the rod needs to be a bit longer (approximately 18.0 cm). Making this piece is a trial-and-error task. In my case, it helped to slowly bend the rod against a circular tube 2 cm in diameter to give it the curve.


Step 5 → Assemble the antenna

We’re already halfway through the process—don’t get discouraged, there’s very little left! Now it’s time to mount the elements onto the cable trunking. This can be replaced by wood or some other material you have on hand, as long as it doesn’t conduct electricity.

On the surface of the trunking, you need to mark the following distances, where each of the rod cuts made in the previous step will be placed later.

  1. Reflector: 1.0 cm
  2. Dipole: 5.0 cm
  3. Director #1: 6.2 cm
  4. Director #2: 9.2 cm
  5. Director #3: 12.8 cm
  6. Director #4: 17.0 cm
  7. Director #5: 21.7 cm
  8. Director #6: 26.7 cm
  9. Director #7: 31.9 cm
  10. Director #8: 37.4 cm

Once the lines are marked, you can glue each of the rods with epoxy glue onto the trunking; however, we’ve seen that this configuration is very fragile and makes our antenna not last very long. For this reason, we found a better way to attach the rods to the trunking. For this, we can use the hot soldering iron, which with its fine tip can easily pierce the plastic. So, with this tool, we are going to drill the sides until they have a diameter large enough for the rod to pass through. Take advantage of the plastic while it’s still hot right after you pierce it to pass the rod through and ensure it has the right diameter. This must be done on both sides of the trunking so the rod can go all the way through.

Once you finish the 20 holes, you should disassemble the trunking and use the metal file to remove the plastic burrs to allow the rods to enter easily.

Now, before positioning each of the rods, we must make a small cut to facilitate the entry of the dipole. For this, we must disassemble the trunking and use the hacksaw to make a vertical cut so that it crosses the sides of the second measurement (5.0 cm) until it reaches the hole we opened with the soldering iron.

Once the dipole is inserted, we can insert the other rods according to the order already mentioned.

You can add a little epoxy glue on the rod to ensure the elements are well adhered to the trunking. Wait for the necessary time for the glue to dry before continuing with connecting the cable to your new antenna.


Step 6 → Prepare the cable

While the glue is drying, you can move forward with preparing the coaxial cable. You can check the previous post to understand how a coaxial cable is made before taking it apart.

To do this part, you can use a wire stripper or a cutter to separate each layer of the cable; you must be careful with the cut so as not to break the first layer, which is a metal mesh. Thus, at one end, you have to leave the copper wire and the cable mesh free. You can carefully separate the mesh and then braid it back together.

At the other end, with the copper wire, make a wave, which will be the element that comes into contact with the phone or MiFi to improve the signal.


Step 7 → Solder the dipole

This is one of the steps that requires the most care; after this, we move on to assembly. Remember that the dipole is one of the fundamental components of the antenna; it must be soldered to the end of the coaxial cable that we separated into the mesh and the copper wire, and this in turn connects with a copper wire acting as a bridge between each end of the dipole. First, we make the bridge with the copper wire as seen in the image, where each end is braided and then soldered.

Once we have the copper wire soldered to the two ends of the coaxial, we can attach it to the dipole; for this, we can first tighten it a little with pliers and then proceed to solder.

Remember that the dipole must be perpendicular to the trunking, so to avoid sudden movements of the cable that change this position, we must secure the cable with some plastic zip ties.


Step 8 → Perform the final assembly

Finally, we’re done! You can now mount and install the antenna! To do this, make sure it is well secured with plastic zip ties at the highest part of the pole; the antenna must be oriented so that the dipole is as close as possible to the pole.

Remember to also tie the cable to the pole so it doesn’t come loose from the solder points on the antenna. Now, you have to hoist the pole and ensure that the position of the antenna is facing the direction of the nearest cell tower.

Now, at the other end of the cable, place your cell phone so that the copper wave is touching the back of the device.

The process of aiming the antenna must be done slowly and carefully; waves are very sensitive to movement. First, you can establish position 0 and, from there, you can turn to the right or left in small intervals. After each movement, you can check the connection quality with the cellular network from the “Settings” screen of the phone. This is a number expressed in decibels (dB); the higher it is, the lower the signal quality. You can also do it by looking at the intensity of the signal icon that appears at the top of the cell phone. The challenge is to find the place where the decibel number is lowest or where the signal intensity icon is highest.

Now, you can proceed to secure the pole in this position and that’s it! Happy surfing!


Recommendations for potential trouble spots

If you are using solder and it keeps slipping, it must be that the tin solder you possibly bought isn’t compatible with the metal of the rods you are using, so we recommend looking for other solder or trying to make contact between the cable and the rod by tightening them well using a wire. Normally, each metal is compatible with solder of the same metal (steel with steel solder, tin with tin solder).

We can know if this solution works by doing speed tests. You can compare by taking measurements with and without the antenna to recognize how much the signal improves with this device.
