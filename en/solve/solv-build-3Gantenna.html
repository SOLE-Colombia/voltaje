Build a homemade 3G antenna

Capture signal with a homemade antenna.

Hi, I’m Nicolás, and for some time now, I’ve been learning how to improve my connectivity. I’m not an expert on the subject, but through internet searches, tutorials, and, above all, trial and error, I’ve learned a lot.

I have already tested and installed several solutions, and today, I’m sharing one of them with you: the homemade 3G antenna. Let’s get started!

What is building a homemade 3G antenna?

Building a 3G antenna means that by gathering and assembling easily accessible materials, you can increase the 3G and 4G signal strength of a cell phone or a MiFi-type modem →.

What is it for?

This homemade antenna will increase the capacity to receive the waves that carry the 4G signal → (mobile internet) in order to improve the connection quality from your cell phone or a shared modem (MiFi →).

What do you need?

  1. 3 mm thick aluminum rod (it must be exactly 3 mm).
  2. 50 cm of plastic cable trunking (ideally 2 cm wide).
  3. Quick-drying epoxy glue (comes in two tubes).
  4. 10 m of coaxial cable (or the length you need for the antenna to reach the connection point).
  5. 2 clamps or vises.
  6. 2 locking pliers.
  7. 20 cm of copper wire (recommended: 1 mm thick).
  8. A ruler (even better if you have a “caliper,” as it allows you to be more precise).
  9. Hacksaw (if you can get an electric grinder with a thin cutting disc, you will save a looot of time).
  10. Fine-tip permanent marker (e.g., Sharpie).
  11. Pencil.
  12. Soldering iron, solder (tin), soldering paste (flux).
  13. Pliers and wire cutters (the latter is optional).
  14. Metal file.
  15. Workbench with good lighting.
  16. Plastic zip ties (also known as “cable ties”).
  17. Pole to mount the antenna (a bamboo pole as long as possible or a rigid rod of any material).
  18. Utility knife, cutter.
  19. Wire stripper (optional).
  20. Cell phone or shared modem (MiFi).
  21. Also… good vibes.
  22. Patience.
  23. Lots of precision.

How to do it?

Step 0 → Before starting, some recommendations for your safety

This solution has an intermediate level of difficulty and involves the use of some tools that can cause accidents if you are not careful. Therefore, it is important to keep the following recommendations in mind:

  • When using any sharp object, position it so that the cutting force is directed away from your body and not toward it.
  • Keep pets or children away from the work area, mainly because of the risk of contact with a hot soldering iron.
  • Before cutting, ensure that the clamps, vises, or pliers holding the material do not allow the slightest movement when applying any type of force.
  • When using solder and epoxy glue, especially protect the work surface so as not to damage it.
  • Do not hoist the antenna during a storm, as the antenna can act as a lightning rod that attracts lightning to your surroundings.

Step 1 → Check the materials

Before starting, check and have all the necessary elements on hand to work. In case you don’t recognize any of the materials, you can find them in the photo where the numbered materials appear.

You can make your purchases at the nearest hardware store as the materials are common; it shouldn’t be too complicated to find them. Perhaps the most difficult (and important) item on the list is the aluminum rod. Sometimes you can find elements made with this type of rod in the metal supports sold in stores that carry many Chinese products. At SOLE Voltaje, we have used different metals: copper, welding rods, and galvanized wire. We chose to buy a 3mm steel welding rod. If you can’t find aluminum at the hardware store, you can ask for other options; the important thing is that its diameter is as close as possible to 3mm or 1/8 of an inch.

Now organize the workspace, have these instructions on hand, and divide the tasks among the participants in the process: someone can be in charge of measuring, others of cutting, organizing, and gluing.


Step 2 → Understand the diagram

Below we show you the final diagram and some of the assembly parts so that you have greater clarity on the final setup.

Step 3 → Measure, cut, and mark**

It’s time to start! Here we get to work; you must cut each of the 10 elements that make up the antenna. With the help of the caliper (or ruler), you have to mark the length of each one and cut them with the hacksaw or the grinder. It is recommended that you cut one or half a millimeter extra and finish adjusting its length by filing the edges with the metal file. Listed below is the length of the 10 metal rods you must cut; in the world of antennas, these have their specific names (director, reflector, and dipole) related to the function they perform.

This is the list of cuts that are necessary:

  1. Reflector: 8.0 cm
  2. Dipole: 18 cm (This should be the length of the element; the bent rod must be exactly 7.6 cm, for now just cut the rod to this length).
  3. Director #1: 7.6 cm
  4. Director #2: 7.5 cm
  5. Director #3: 7.4 cm
  6. Director #4: 7.4 cm
  7. Director #5: 7.3 cm
  8. Director #6: 7.3 cm
  9. Director #7: 7.2 cm
  10. Director #8: 7.2 cm

📏 Remember that 10 mm = 1 cm

It is important to mark each of the cuts with tape, as seen in the photo, so that the next step is easier to do.


Step 4 → Bend the dipole

The dipole is an element bent into a “C” shape. Its total length must be 7.6 cm, but the rod must be a little longer (approximately 18.0 cm). Making this piece is a trial-and-error task. In my case, it has helped to slowly bend the rod against a circular tube 2 cm in diameter to give it the curvature.


Step 5 → Assembling the antenna

We are reaching the middle of the process. Don’t get discouraged, there’s very little left! Now it’s time to assemble the elements onto the cable trunking. This can be replaced by wood or some other material on hand, as long as it does not conduct electricity.

On the surface of the trunking, you have to mark the following distances, on which each of the rod cuts made in the previous step will subsequently be placed.

  1. Reflector: 1.0 cm
  2. Dipole: 5.0 cm
  3. Director #1: 6.2 cm
  4. Director #2: 9.2 cm
  5. Director #3: 12.8 cm
  6. Director #4: 17.0 cm
  7. Director #5: 21.7 cm
  8. Director #6: 26.7 cm
  9. Director #7: 31.9 cm
  10. Director #8: 37.4 cm

Once the lines are marked, you can glue each of the rods with the epoxy glue onto the trunking; however, we have seen that this configuration is very fragile and makes our antenna not last very long. For this reason, we found a better way to attach the rods to the trunking. For this, we can use the hot soldering iron, which with its fine tip can easily pierce the plastic, so with this tool, we are going to drill the sides until it has a diameter sufficient for the rod to pass through. Take advantage of the plastic while it is still hot after you pierce it to pass the rod through and ensure it has the appropriate diameter. This should be done on both sides of the trunking so that the rod can pass from side to side.

Once you finish the 20 perforations, you must disassemble the trunking and use the metal file to remove the plastic burr to allow the rods to enter easily.

Now, before positioning each of the rods, we must make a small cut to facilitate the entry of the dipole; for this, we must disassemble the trunking and use the hacksaw to make a vertical cut so that it crosses the sides of the second measurement (5.0 cm) until it reaches the perforation we opened with the soldering iron.

Once the dipole is embedded, we can insert the other rods according to the order already mentioned.

You can add a little epoxy glue on the rod to ensure that the elements remain well adhered to the trunking. Wait for the necessary time for the glue to dry before continuing with connecting the cable to your new antenna.


Step 6 → Prepare the cable

While the glue is drying, you can proceed with the preparation of the coaxial cable. You can check the previous entry to understand how a coaxial cable is made before taking it apart.

To do this part, you can use a wire stripper or a cutter to separate each layer of the cable; you must be careful with the cut so as not to break the first layer, which is a metal mesh. Thus, at one end, you have to leave the copper wire and the cable mesh free. You can separate the mesh carefully and then braid it again.

At the other end, with the copper wire, make a wave, which will be the element that comes into contact with the phone, or MiFi, to improve the signal.


Step 7 → Solder the dipole

This is one of the most careful steps; after this, we move on to assembly. Remember that the dipole is one of the fundamental components of the antenna; it must be soldered to the end of the coaxial cable that we separated into the mesh and the copper wire, and this in turn connects with a copper wire acting as a bridge between each end of the dipole. First, we make the bridge with the copper wire as seen in the image, where each end is braided and then soldered.

Once we have the copper wire soldered to the two ends of the coaxial, we can couple it to the dipole; for this, we can first tighten it a little with the pliers and then proceed to solder.

Remember that the dipole must be perpendicular to the trunking, so to avoid sudden movements of the cable that change this position, we must secure the cable with plastic zip ties.


Step 8 → Final assembly

Finally, we are done! You can now perform the assembly and installation of the antenna! To do this, make sure it is well adjusted with plastic zip ties at the highest part of the pole; the antenna must be oriented in such a way that the dipole is as close as possible to the pole.

Remember to also tie the cable to the pole so that it does not come loose from the solder points on the antenna. Now, you have to hoist the pole and make sure that the position of the antenna is looking in the direction of the nearest cell tower.

Now, at the other end of the cable, place your cell phone so that the copper serpentine is touching the back of the device.

The antenna aiming process must be done slowly and carefully; the waves are very sensitive to movement. First, you can establish the 0 position, and from there, you can go turning to the right or left in small intervals. After each movement, you can check the connection quality with the cellular network from the “Settings” screen of the phone. This is a number expressed in decibels (dB); the higher it is, the lower the signal quality will be. You can also do it by looking at the intensity of the signal icon that appears at the top of the cell phone. The challenge is to find the place where the decibel number is lowest or where the signal intensity icon is greatest.

Now, you can proceed to secure the pole in this position and that’s it! Happy browsing!


Recommendations for potential difficulty points

If you are using solder and it slips continuously, it must be that the tin solder you possibly bought is not compatible with the metal of the rods you are using, so we recommend looking for another solder or trying to make contact between the cable and the rod by tightening them well with a wire. Normally each metal is compatible with a solder of the same metal (steel with steel solder, tin with tin solder).
