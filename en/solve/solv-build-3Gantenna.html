Build a DIY 3G antenna

Boost signal with a homemade antenna.

Hi, I’m Nicolás, and for a while now, I’ve been learning how to improve my connectivity. I’m no expert, but through internet searches, tutorials, and, above all, trial and error, I’ve learned a lot.

I’ve already tested and installed several solutions, and today, I’m sharing one of them with you: the DIY 3G antenna. Let’s get to it!

What is building a DIY 3G antenna?

Building a 3G antenna means that by gathering and assembling easily accessible materials, you can boost the strength of the 3G and 4G signal for a cell phone or a MiFi-type modem.

What is it for?

This DIY antenna will increase the ability to receive the waves that carry the 4G signal (mobile internet), thereby improving the quality of the connection from your cell phone or a shared modem (MiFi).

What do you need?

  1. 3 mm thick aluminum rod (it has to be exactly 3mm).
  2. 50 cm of plastic conduit (ideally 2cm wide).
  3. Quick-drying epoxy glue (comes in two tubes).
  4. 10 m of coaxial cable (or the length you need for the antenna to reach the connection point).
  5. 2 clamps or vises.
  6. 2 locking pliers.
  7. 20 cm of copper wire (recommended: 1mm thick).
  8. A ruler (even better if you have a “caliper,” which allows you to be more precise).
  9. Hacksaw (if you can get an electric angle grinder with a thin cutting disc, you’ll save a looot of time).
  10. Fine-tip permanent marker (e.g., Sharpie).
  11. Pencil.
  12. Soldering iron, solder (tin), soldering paste (flux).
  13. Pliers and wire cutters (the latter is optional).
  14. Metal file.
  15. Workbench with good lighting.
  16. Plastic ties (also known as “zip ties”).
  17. Pole to mount the antenna (a bamboo pole as long as possible or a rigid rod of any material).
  18. Utility knife, cutter.
  19. Wire strippers (optional).
  20. Cell phone or shared modem (MiFi).
  21. Plus… good vibes.
  22. Patience.
  23. Lots of precision.

How to do it?

Step 0 → Follow these safety recommendations

This solution has an intermediate level of difficulty and involves using some tools that can cause accidents if you aren’t careful. That’s why it’s important to keep the following recommendations in mind:

  • When using any sharp object, position it so that the cutting force is directed away from your body, not toward it.
  • Keep pets or children away from the work area, mainly because of the risk of contact with a hot soldering iron.
  • Before cutting, make sure the clamps, vises, or pliers holding the material don’t allow it to move even slightly when you apply force.
  • When using solder and epoxy glue, protect the work surface to avoid damaging it.
  • Do not hoist the antenna during storms, as the antenna could act as a lightning rod, attracting a strike to your surroundings.

Step 1 → Check the materials

Before starting, check and have all the necessary items on hand. In case you don’t recognize any of the materials, you can find them in the photo where the numbered materials appear.

You can buy them at the nearest hardware store since these are common materials; it shouldn’t be too hard to find them. Perhaps the most difficult (and important) item on the list is the aluminum rod. Sometimes you can find items made with this type of rod in the metal supports sold at discount stores that carry many imported products. At SOLE Voltaje, we have used different metals: copper, welding rods, and galvanized wire. We chose to buy a 3mm steel welding rod. If you can’t find aluminum at the hardware store, you can ask for other options; the important thing is that its diameter is as close as possible to 3mm or 1/8 of an inch.

Now, organize your workspace, keep these instructions handy, and divide the tasks among the people involved in the process: someone can be in charge of measuring, others of cutting, organizing, and gluing.

Step 3 → Measure, cut, and mark

It’s time to start! Now we get down to business; you must cut each of the 10 elements that make up the antenna. With the help of the caliper (or ruler), you have to mark the length of each one and cut them with the hacksaw or the angle grinder. It’s recommended to cut a millimeter or half a millimeter extra and finish adjusting the length by filing the edges with the metal file. Listed below are the lengths of the 10 metal rods you need to cut; in the world of antennas, these have specific names (director, reflector, and dipole) related to the function they perform.

This is the list of necessary cuts:

  1. Reflector: 8.0 cm
  2. Dipole: 18 cm (This should be the length of the element; the bent rod must be exactly 7.6 cm, for now, just cut the rod to this length).
  3. Director #1: 7.6 cm
  4. Director #2: 7.5 cm
  5. Director #3: 7.4 cm
  6. Director #4: 7.4 cm
  7. Director #5: 7.3 cm
  8. Director #6: 7.3 cm
  9. Director #7: 7.2 cm
  10. Director #8: 7.2 cm

📏 Remember that 10 mm = 1 cm

It’s important to mark each of the cuts with tape, as seen in the photo, to make the next step easier.


Step 4 → Bend the dipole

The dipole is an element bent into a “C” shape. Its total length should be 7.6 cm, but the rod needs to be a bit longer (approximately 18.0 cm). Making this piece is a trial-and-error task. In my case, it helped to slowly bend the rod against a circular tube 2 cm in diameter to give it the curvature.


Step 5 → Assemble the antenna

We’re halfway through the process—don’t get discouraged, we’re almost there! Now it’s time to mount the elements onto the conduit. This can be replaced by wood or some other material you have on hand, as long as it doesn’t conduct electricity.

On the surface of the conduit, you need to mark the following distances, where each of the rod cuts made in the previous step will be placed later.

  1. Reflector: 1.0 cm
  2. Dipole: 5.0 cm
  3. Director #1: 6.2 cm
  4. Director #2: 9.2 cm
  5. Director #3: 12.8 cm
  6. Director #4: 17.0 cm
  7. Director #5: 21.7 cm
  8. Director #6: 26.7 cm
  9. Director #7: 31.9 cm
  10. Director #8: 37.4 cm

Once the lines are marked, you can glue each of the rods with epoxy glue onto the conduit; however, we’ve seen that this configuration is very fragile and makes our antenna not last very long. For this reason, we found a better way to attach the rods to the conduit. For this, we can use the hot soldering iron, which can easily pierce the plastic with its fine tip; so, with this tool, we are going to drill the sides until they have a large enough diameter for the rod to pass through. Take advantage of the plastic while it’s still hot right after you pierce it to pass the rod through and ensure it has the right diameter. This must be done on both sides of the conduit so the rod can go all the way through.

Once you finish the 20 holes, you should disassemble the conduit and use the metal file to remove the plastic burrs to allow the rods to enter easily.
