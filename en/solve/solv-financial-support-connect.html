  • Mintic - “Connecting the Unconnected” program by MINTIC and Colnodo

The “Connecting the Unconnected” program is an initiative of the Ministry of Information and Communications Technologies (MinTIC) of Colombia, which seeks to bring the Internet to rural areas and communities with little or no connectivity.

The Digital Centers are free Wi-Fi access points installed in strategic locations to facilitate internet access in these areas.

Steps to access, according to the program’s website:

  1. Locate a Digital Center: Digital Centers are located in rural areas and communities with little connectivity. You can find information about their location on the MinTIC website.
  2. Turn on your device’s Wi-Fi: Turn on the Wi-Fi on your phone, tablet, or other device you want to connect.
  3. Search for the “MinTIC Conecta” network: Your device will show available Wi-Fi networks. Select the network named “Mintic Conecta”.
  4. Fill in the requested data: You will be asked to enter some information to register. Fill in the required fields.
  5. Accept the terms and conditions: Read the terms and conditions carefully and, if you agree, accept them to connect. Done! Once the previous steps are completed, you will be able to use the Internet connection.
  • Colnodo

In Colombia, access to the 900 MHz band for communities is being addressed through a public consultation led by the Ministry of Information and Communications Technologies (MinTIC) and the National Spectrum Agency (ANE).

The objective is to make the use of this band more flexible for community networks, especially in rural areas with little or no commercial network coverage.

Process and Participation:

  • Public Consultation: A public consultation has been opened so that organized communities, community networks, and other interested actors can express their interest and provide comments on the proposal.
  • Participation Form: An online form is available for communities to describe their situation, interests, and suggestions regarding the use of the band.
  • Information and Documentation: The ANE has published documents and guidelines for the consultation, which can be viewed and downloaded.
  • Deadline: The public consultation is open until July 1, 2025.

Focus on Rural Areas:

  • The 900 MHz band, with its capacity for good coverage and penetration, is considered ideal for supporting connectivity in rural and remote areas.
  • The goal is for community networks to be able to leverage this band to bring connectivity to places where there is no commercial coverage or it is limited.

Potential Benefits:

  • Community Connectivity: Facilitate internet access for rural communities through community networks.
  • Economic and Social Development: Boost access to services such as telemedicine, virtual education, and other opportunities thanks to connectivity.
  • Community Empowerment: Community networks can generate experience and autonomy in managing their own networks.
  • Future Readiness: Experience with this band can pave the way for the adoption of more advanced technologies and services in the future.

Active Participation:

It is crucial that interested communities actively participate in the public consultation, expressing their needs, expectations, and proposals for the use of the 900 MHz band. The participation of all interested sectors is fundamental to building a strategy that guarantees fair and sustainable connectivity for everyone.

In summary, the 900 MHz band represents an opportunity for Colombian communities to access the internet and improve their connectivity, especially in rural areas, through community networks.

The public consultation is the space for communities to express their needs and contribute to the construction of a public policy that recognizes the importance of the spectrum for social and economic development.

  • NUPEF

NUPEF.org, through its focus on community networks and affordable internet access, can facilitate access to funding and resources to build or strengthen community internet networks, in addition to offering training to manage and maintain these networks.

It also promotes the creation of safe and inclusive virtual communities, empowering marginalized groups to use technology and advocate for their digital rights:

  • It can help you apply for grants and funding for community network projects, including building new access solutions or expanding existing ones, especially for marginalized and underserved populations.

  • Offers training and resources so that community members develop skills in building, managing, and maintaining community networks.

  • If you already have a network, NUPEF can help strengthen it by improving infrastructure, updating technology, and providing technical support to ensure its continuous operation.

  • Promotes the creation of safe and welcoming online communities, where diversity is valued and users are protected from violence and abuse.

  • Works so that excluded people can use and adapt the internet and digital technology for their needs, including the development of fair and equitable solutions for universal access.

  • Seeks economic models that allow for affordable and sustainable internet access, generating value in the community and promoting self-sufficiency.

  • Facilitates the exchange of knowledge and experiences between different communities and movements, promoting collective action for digital inclusion.

How to know if this solution works?

How having low-cost internet can help:

  • Infrastructure funding: there are organizations that offer funding to organizations that build and expand internet networks in underserved areas, which can result in more affordable options for residents.
  • Information on subsidy programs: other organizations provide information on government and non-government programs that offer subsidies or reduced rates for internet access for low-income individuals.
  • Community networks: others support the creation of community networks, which are often a more economical alternative to commercial options.
  • Resources to build networks: some others offer a toolkit for building and maintaining community networks, which can help reduce infrastructure costs.
  • Training for the installation and maintenance of equipment and networks.
  • Strengthening the social fabric and bonds between the community and the community of practice: initiatives usually include content and practice in digital rights, equity, community ties, and human connection, which make the internet an excuse to have more conscious and connected communities.
