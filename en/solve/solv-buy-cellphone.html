¿Cómo comprar un celular adecuado para mí?

Un celular que se ajuste a tus capacidades y necesidades.

Hi, I’m Nicolás, and just like you, I’ve been learning step-by-step how to improve my connectivity to do SOLE. I’m no expert, but through searches on Internet →, tutorials, and, above all, trial and error, I’ve learned a ton and set up some functional solutions.

What does it mean to buy the right cell phone for you?

In this specific solution, we’ll learn how to buy a Smartphone → based on your possibilities and needs. Below, you’ll find a series of information, criteria, and tips that aim to be as clear as possible so you can feel supported in making the decision of which phone to buy. The estimated reading time is 12 minutes and it consists of 10 items, but don’t be scared—you can read these items with the help of others. Remember that at SOLE Voltaje, we want to promote the collective use of the internet.

What is it for?

In this solution, you’ll find a few practical tips to help you make a much more informed decision about which cell phones are best for you.

Before we start, some ethical questions!

At SOLE Voltaje, we talk about supporting people in clarifying the ethical implications of a decision by problematizing the circumstances that lead you to make that decision. This solution starts from the assumption that you want to buy a cell phone—but do you really need it? This isn’t always the best option for you. Sometimes you have an old phone that a family member can give you, or for various reasons, you might prefer not to have a phone and it’s a better option for you to have a computer. If, after scanning your options, you definitely decide you need to buy a phone, this is the right place for you! Here, we advise you on what you should keep in mind when buying a cell phone.

What do you need?

  • Save money for the purchase.
  • Internet access.
  • A trusted store or dealer.
  • Curiosity.

How to do it?

Step 0 → How do I know if I need to buy a cell phone?

One of the characteristics of our current era is the great development of international and national markets; our phones are made in China and we can buy them in Colombia, we can go to the market and buy rice brought from Brazil. Furthermore, we find disproportionate consumption options: we can buy white rice, yellow rice, bulk rice, lemon-flavored rice, etc. There is an endless array of options to turn us into consumers of products we often don’t need, and we end up buying non-nutritious food, falling into yet another expense that doesn’t satisfy the main function of food: to nourish us! In the same way, we fill our homes with endless merchandise that ends up piled up or thrown away because, in the end, it was useless to us. That’s why we want to promote responsible consumption. For the case of a cell phone, we suggest you ask yourself the following questions to know if it’s a responsible purchase:

  • Do you need to buy a cell phone, or do you have other options to access the internet and make calls?
  • Can you buy another option that covers your internet connection and calls, and can be paid for by several people?
  • If you buy a cell phone, will your quality of life really improve, or will it get worse? For example, will you become addicted to watching content or being on social media, or will you be more stressed every month thinking about how to pay for this new expense?
  • Finally, is this new purchase really a priority for you?

Step 1 → What do I need a cell phone for?

Just like a car, the phone we choose must adapt to our main needs. It doesn’t make much sense to buy a 4x4 truck if you’re going to be driving through a city with paved avenues.

Now, think about what the most important activities will be and where you will spend the most time on your phone: Do you want to take lots of photos of your region? Then you should pay attention to the camera features; Are you going to be out in the middle of the countryside all day, or are you going to use your phone as a modem to share your internet with other people participating in a SOLE? A good battery would be fundamental; Do you want to use the internet to learn in a group? A large screen with good definition is important; Do you want to play a high-definition video game? You’ll need a good processor →; Do you need to work with several applications at once? Look at the RAM memory →; Do you need to have many applications and large files? Look at the internal storage.

Now that you’ve decided to buy a phone, I’ll give you an introduction using my own case as an example:

💡 At the beginning of 2024, I dropped my phone getting out of a taxi. Because of my work and contact with family and friends, it’s important for me to have a phone. A while ago, I decided I wouldn’t invest more than 700,000 COP.

However, I searched online for references and saw that there’s a new brand arriving that is affordable: Tecno. I looked at several references and saw that it’s currently the best-selling brand on Mercado Libre, so I looked for a phone with similar characteristics to the previous ones: 256 GB of internal memory, a decent processor, a battery of over 4000 mAh, a large screen, and at least 8 GB of RAM. These characteristics are compatible with my work and personal use; I don’t take professional photos, nor was I looking for it to play video games. Finally, I found a phone from this brand for $475,000 COP. This phone I bought was a Tecno Spark 20 Dual Sim with 256 GB. So far, I can say it has been an excellent purchase!

Now it’s your turn to identify what you need the phone for!

Step 2 → Set a budget

First of all, define what your budget will be. For this, you can calculate how many days of work you are willing to invest in this device. Don’t go into debt for a phone; many times they offer financing for 1 or 2 years, and the phone breaks or gets stolen before we finish paying for it.

Once you are clear on the above, you will know which features you should pay more attention to.

  • Camera If you love taking photos, here are some aspects that are central when examining your device’s camera:

  • Resolution This is what we commonly know as megapixels. Although it is very important, it is not everything we should look at when evaluating a camera. The more megapixels it has, the more detail you will see in the images. However, you should have a limit. The standard could be a 12 MP camera, because if it has good features and excellent image processing, you will get high-quality photos. If you start having higher resolution photos, the files will be very heavy and will fill up your phone’s internal storage quickly.

  • Sensor and lenses A good cell phone camera must have a quality sensor, as well as several lenses. The latter, in fact, has already become a standard. When combined, they allow for better images. One of the most popular lenses is the wide-angle lens. Thanks to this, much more information is incorporated into the photographs and it provides a visual angle of up to 120 degrees, perfect for group photos or indoors where there isn’t much space.

  • Aperture The lens aperture will be fundamental for a good photo; this feature defines how much light the camera can capture. It is identified by the letter f followed by a number, for example, f1.9 or f2.0. The lower this number, the greater the amount of light the camera will capture. For this reason, it is important that it be low, because with more light intake, the images will have more detail.

  • Screen A screen will define whether our eyes get tired faster or slower, whether we will see a certain degree of detail or not, or how fast the battery will drain.

When you are clear that your phone should be a device used in a group, as happens when we do SOLE, this feature becomes important, since it is not so easy for several people to read on a small screen.

  • Screen size The size of cell phone screens has increased over time, although for a few years now they have remained without growing much, since they lose their portability function in the pocket. We could say that most phones are between 3.5” and 6” (this measures the diagonal, that is, from one upper corner to the opposite lower corner). Really, this depends on your taste: do you want a large screen that allows you to watch a video from afar among several people, or do you prefer a small one so your phone doesn’t attract so much attention?

  • Screen technology Full HD technology is more recent than HD, and the number of pixels is higher compared to HD, which works with 1,280 x 720 pixels. The Full HD system gives a much more realistic look to the image. This latest technology is only arriving in the latest phones with large panels, as high resolution doesn’t make much sense on small screens.

  • Screen type There are different types of screens, but in the current market, two are the most popular: those with LCD technology, also known as inorganic, and those with OLED technologies, known as organic.

  • LCD: These are backlit liquid crystal screens that usually cover the entire panel. In the past, they were the most popular, but they are being produced in smaller quantities. Being backlit, they will consume more energy, but for this very reason, they look good under direct sunlight.

  • OLED: These screens differ from LCDs because their materials allow them to generate light by themselves when electricity is applied. This allows them to turn each pixel off or on independently, so they consume less energy. Furthermore, they end up being brighter and having better definition of the color black.

  • Internal storage Internal storage defines the amount of files we can save on our phone; it’s like the hard drive → in a computer. This will determine how many programs you can have installed at once, and whether or not you can save all kinds of files. At the moment, we recommend buying a phone with at least 64 GB of internal memory. Also, check if it has the option to accept microSD cards to expand the capacity in the future.

  • RAM memory This is the phone’s temporary memory; it helps with performance and gives a clue as to how fast applications open and how smoothly the phone runs. We recommend a minimum of 4GB of RAM.

  • Processor The processor is the brain; it is the one that is always awake and receives instructions. The more cores the processor has, the better performance it will have, although it will also depend on the brand. Here, the best option is to look at the name of the processor used by the phone that catches your attention and do a little research on your own. Hopefully, it hasn’t been out for more than 4 years.

Don’t be fooled

It is important that you constantly check emerging brands; usually, brands appear with little advertising, very good features, and a low price. However, many times out of habit and experience, we keep buying the same brand as our previous phone. For example, my best friend has been with the Motorola brand since college. I think that although it is a good brand, it has very high costs, and I prefer new, cheaper brands that have good references to save some money.

Another important element has to do with the warranty. When you live in a central city, it’s easy that if something happens to the phone, there is a branch of all available brands in case you have to use the warranty. But in less central cities, for example in Bucaramanga, the brand of my current phone, “Tecno,” doesn’t have an office, so if you live there, you would have to send the phone to Bogotá, pay more money, and wait longer for them to return it. But possibly in that city, there is a service center for more popular brands, as is the case with Motorola.

How much is enough?

This question is difficult to answer, but I think we can rephrase it as follows: Does the option you chose meet the need and intention you projected at the beginning? In the previous example, I showed you how I consider that the phone I currently have was a very good option for my lifestyle. This type of reasoning is what we want you to do when making a decision about the option you are going to buy.
