How to write an invitation that engages?

Attract the attention of the people you need.

Create your own invitation

Hi there! 🙂 I’m Manu, the storyteller from SOLE Colombia.

In my day-to-day, I’ve had to put together plenty of invitations, so I’m just the right person to help you craft one that really hits the mark.

What makes a good invitation?

A good invitation is one that gives the guest the right info, sparks interest, makes it easy to RSVP, and has a design that fits the vibe of the event.

Writing an invitation is a challenge. Staring at a blank page and not knowing where to start is something even the best-selling authors struggle with.

So, take it easy—you’re not alone.

With a few tips and a bit of creativity, we can make sure your SOLE, meetings, projects, or initiatives send a clear message so your guests will say “yes” to your invite.

What is this for?

Follow these recommendations or tips whenever you need to send an invite. If you’re feeling lost and don’t know where to start, I recommend heading over to Create a powerful message first. That’ll give you more tools to build your invitation.

Are you ready to learn how to win people over with words and make your next event a total success? Let’s put together a powerful invitation!

  • ATTENTION: I can’t guarantee people will be on time, but I can guarantee their confirmation.

What do you need?

  • Paper.
  • Pen/Pencil.
  • Enthusiasm.
  • Cell phone or computer (you can write your invite there, too).

How to do it?

  • Moment 1: The content

It’s super important that all your invitations make the what, how, and where of your plans crystal clear.

Let’s get to work:

  • Grab a blank sheet of paper and write down what you’re going to do at your gathering. This could be anything from watching a movie, discussing an important topic in your community, or hosting a SOLE.
  • Next, answer how you’re going to do it, for example: a film forum, a meeting at the community hall, or starting a community work group.
  • Now, you just need to write the where: at your house, at the Arroyito neighborhood community hall, at the Luis Ángel Arango Library… whatever space you’ve planned, and that’s it!

Don’t worry if it doesn’t come out perfect the first time; look at how many times I had to erase in the video to make my invitation… we’ll fine-tune it as we go step-by-step!

  • Moment 2: The writing Great! Now it’s time to let all that creativity flow onto the paper. With the content clear, it’ll be much easier to write the invitation.

“The hook,” how to grab attention In the world of invitations, the first sentence is key. That’s why there are different techniques to grab people’s attention so they feel engaged in those first few seconds of reading.

💡 Questions: Starting sentences with direct questions is a popular option on social media to grab the viewer’s attention immediately. For example: Are you up for joining this meeting? or Would you like to rally your community?

💡 The facts: “After more than 60,000 deaths in Palestine, we ask ourselves what the UN is for.” That’s how an article from an alternative media outlet about the conflict in Palestine begins. How about starting your invitation with relevant facts about the main topic of your meeting?

💡 Powerful phrases: You can also just choose a powerful, concise, and clear phrase that shows what your invitation is about at a single glance. Even if it seems a bit obvious, you could use the phrase: “I invite you to (activity you’re going to do)” to call your participants.

I went with this last option. Look at what I wrote:

  • ‘The polisher’, how to fine-tune the message

Here we get straight to the point.

Get to work:

  1. Underline the answers to the how and where questions (the ones we wrote in the ‘Content’ section, a bit further up in this Solution).
  2. Now, write the clear details for the day, time, date, and place where you’re going to gather your people (no fluff).

Put what you wrote for your how in the most summarized and clear way possible. Example: If your how was a meeting at the community hall, put: “Community hall of [Name of your community]“.

  • ‘The action’, what you ask your audience to do right now

Congrats! With this step, we’ll finish writing the invitation. Keep it up! You’re almost there!

Almost always, when we read something, we tend to stick with the last sentence. Often, we only remember the end of a book, a story, or a news piece. That’s why the way your invitation ends is SO IMPORTANT, because that’s how we’re going to move our guests to action.

Activity: Use an action verb to start the sentence that closes your invitation.

Don’t underestimate the power of a good invitation

When you need to make an invitation, don’t forget that the most important thing is to trust your own skills; you know your people better than anyone. You’ll be surprised by the power of well-chosen words to inspire and bring the community together!

Good luck 😉
