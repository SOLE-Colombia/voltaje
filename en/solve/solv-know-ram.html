"How to know how much RAM I have?"

"Learn how to check your computer's memory to see if it is ready for new tasks."

Hi! I’m David. I’ve managed to get a better handle on how my gear works through trial and error, and today I want to share a quick way to peek under the hood of your computer. We’ll lean on what we already know and confirm a few steps with some digital tools so you can’t go wrong.

What is RAM?

RAM is like a kitchen assistant with a short-term memory. Imagine your computer is a kitchen: the RAM is the countertop.

The assistant is super fast at handing you the ingredients you need right now on the counter, but as soon as you turn off the stove (the computer), she clears everything off and forgets what she was doing.

If your counter is too small (low RAM), the assistant gets crowded with pots and pans, and everything slows down. That’s why knowing how much memory you have is key to keeping your gear from getting bogged down.

What is it for?

Knowing how much RAM you have lets you figure out if your computer can handle running many programs at once or if it’s going to “choke” on heavy tasks like videos or games.

What do you need?

  • Your computer (Windows or Mac) turned on.
  • A couple of minutes of peace and quiet.

How to do it?

First, identify if your machine is Windows or Mac.

On Windows (10 and 11)

Step 1 → The quick way: Settings

Press the Windows + i keys on your keyboard (the ‘i’ stands for information). A settings window will pop up.

  • If you have Windows 10: Go to “System” and look for “About” at the bottom of the left-hand menu.
  • If you have Windows 11: Go to “System” and look for the “About” option at the end of the list.

Look for where it says “Installed RAM”. You’ll see a number there like 4GB, 8GB, or 16GB.

Step 2 → The administrator’s path (to see real-time usage)

If you want to see how much work the “assistant” has on her plate right now:

  1. Press Ctrl + Shift + Esc all at once.
  2. Click on the Performance tab and then on Memory.

Here you’ll see how much memory you’re using and how much is left over.

You’re on the right track: If you see the graph is very high (near the top), it means your “countertop” is full of pots and dishes.

On Mac computers (Apple)

Step 1 → The Apple menu

Click the Apple icon () in the top-left corner of your screen and select “About This Mac”.

A small window will open where you’ll clearly see the word Memory.

Step 2 → Using the Terminal (for the curious)

If you want to feel like a pro, you can use the Terminal.

  1. Open the Terminal (find it in Spotlight with Cmd + Space).
  2. Type the command: vm_stat and press Enter.

This will show you a lot of technical info, including the use of Swap Memory.

What is Swap Memory? It’s like when the assistant runs out of space on the counter and starts using a drawer in the pantry (the hard drive) to store dishes. It’s slower than the counter, but it helps keep the kitchen from coming to a complete halt.

Recommendations…

  • If you have 4GB of RAM, try not to have too many browser tabs open at the same time.
  • With 8GB, you can comfortably handle most office and study tasks.
  • If you notice your machine is always using “Swap,” it might be time to think about upgrading your physical RAM or switching to an SSD if your computer allows it.

How do you know if this solution works?

If you managed to find the number (e.g., 8 GB) and understand whether you’re using a lot or a little, congratulations! You know your work tool a bit better now.

Other possible Internets

By knowing how much RAM you have, you stop getting frustrated when the computer slows down and start making decisions: Should I close this program? Do I need to upgrade my gear? Can I install this game?
