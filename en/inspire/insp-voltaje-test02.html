"SOLE Voltage Test #02"

Protocol for test 02 based on learnings from test 01.

This document aims to outline the protocol for conducting Test 02 of the Solutions Guide, based on the lessons and findings from Test 01, which took place on 03.02.2024.

  1. What did we learn from Test 01?
  • Focus the drafting of the solutions on improving writing style, language clarity, and the logical order of instructions.
  • It is important that the solution’s tone makes participants feel supported through every step.
  • It is essential to understand that well-written steps depend on the strategic use of images, diagrams, or photographs to provide key and relevant information.
  • Efforts should be focused on improving: 1. the images accompanying each instruction; 2. the order, clarity, and organization of the instructions.
  • It is essential to have an initial section covering the safety conditions that must be ensured before starting the test.
  • Before starting the solutions (for the Guide), perhaps suggest that people find a comfortable, quiet, and safe space.
  • Participants mentioned that the soda can antenna solution was more motivating than challenging, while the homemade antenna solution felt more like a challenge/frustration than motivation. We need to figure out how to manage frustration.
  • The tone of the guide should be one of companionship and compassion; a tone that makes it clear that the solutions are not for experts and that anyone interested can do them. This tone is currently unclear. The guide’s ethical and political commitment should be evident in this tone. There is no clear communication strategy.
  • It is worth reviewing how typography, font size, and layout facilitate reading. It would be worth testing with people who have difficulty reading on a computer. Also, test the layout with a column-based distribution.
  1. Progress: the solutions must make participants feel that they are moving forward. Participants must know where they are in the test and how much is left.
  2. Companionship: the solutions must be written in such a way that participants feel accompanied through a colloquial and approachable tone. Strategy:
  3. Confirmation: the solutions must assure participants that they are on the right track and that their progress is indeed correct.

Types of images to be used for the tests

  1. Result images: show the final result of the step or the solution being performed. These images allow participants to compare their progress with what the test requires.
  2. Process images: show the steps necessary to reach a result or a specific step.
  3. Context images: show the solutions with people in specific contexts.

Participant profiles

The following profiles can overlap. For example, there are people who may have very little knowledge about how the internet works, but may be very interested in learning for the test.

Interest: people interested in learning how to carry out a solution.Need: people who need the solutions to solve concrete problems.
Knowledge: people who know a lot about how the internet works, or any of the guide’s pillars: electricity, space, community outreach, space management, or connectivity.Skills: people who have soft skills and/or motor skills to carry out the solutions.

2. Objectives of Test 02:

  1. Evaluate the progress made on the solutions based on the lessons from Test 01.
  2. Verify the changes produced in participants by the audio-reading format for the solutions.
  3. Receive feedback on the solutions regarding “how to convene your community” and find the differences and similarities with the “the internet signal is bad” tests.

3. Questions to formulate Test 02:

  1. Participant profiles: Based on the lessons and objectives, who and how many people should be part of the test? How do we divide the test for the profiles of interest, need, knowledge, and skills?
  2. Test format: Should we carry out the solution virtually and remotely? What is the reason we should do it this way? Does remote mean doing the test with a synchronous observer or, conversely, giving a time frame of a week and a half (for example) to complete the test?
  3. Care: What considerations and care should we have for the people who will perform these tests?
  4. Expectations: What do we expect from the people who will perform the test?
  5. Feedback/evaluation: How will we receive feedback/evaluation from the participants?
  6. Roles: What roles within the SOLE team are necessary to carry out this test?

4. Proposal for Test 02 (answers to the questions in point 3):

  1. Participant profiles: Test 02 will be carried out by participants remotely and virtually.
  • SOLUTION ITERATION TEST: evaluate if the rewriting of the Test 01 solutions improved the experience. Two of the participants from Test 01 could repeat the tests performed in Test 01.
    • READING TEST with Audio-reading (remote 1 week): With the initial 4 people, both homemade antenna and soda can.
    • EXECUTION TEST (remote 1 week): Invite 2 new people to execute the homemade antenna test. 2 new people for the soda can one.
  • SOLUTION START TEST: check the progress we have made for people who have not had contact with the Solutions Guide. Two new people could work on one or two solutions different from those prototyped in Test 01.
    • REMOTE 3G antenna installation: 2 Spinner Explorers.
    • IN-PERSON “Restart and share your phone’s internet with others.”
  • COMPARISON TEST BETWEEN CATEGORIES: check the necessary changes between the tests on “how to convene your community” and “the internet signal is bad.” The other two participants from Test 01 could work on the two new tests on “how to convene your community.”
  1. Test format: Participants will have an estimated time of 1 week and a half to send the test results; this will allow us to evaluate, organically, how much interest they had in developing the test.
  2. Care: Since it will be virtual, give people time to perform the test at their own pace. Make people feel calm, comfortable, and be honest with SOLE, both to say they don’t want to do the test and to provide feedback and evaluate the solutions.
  3. Expectations:
  4. Feedback/evaluation:

  1. an entry form (before starting the test) and another upon finishing the test.

  2. I suggest using the self-documentation method so that, during week 01, people can record their experience with the test.

Methodology

  3. Send test instructions.
  4. If the community proposes conversation and questions, we accompany them.
  5. Ask for final feedback (pending to see if it is synchronous and group-based or not).
  6. Thank them for their participation.

Lessons

  • Of the 7 people who received the antenna, only one decided to contact SOLE to ask for support with the installation.
  • Antennas cannot be delivered again in communities without a support process and making the value and potential of them explicit.
  • We have to do a test, yes or yes, in the contacts with people to generate the habit.
