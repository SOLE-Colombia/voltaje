"Test #03 SOLE Voltage"

Workshop to reflect on our technological autonomy.

Common Ground Workshop

Title: Creating devices to reflect on our technological autonomy

Facilitators: Nicolás Gaitán; Manuela Cordoba and Mateo Acosta, SOLE Colombia Foundation

Duration: 90 minutes

Description: First, analog data visualization devices will be installed to spark reflection and dialogue among participants. Building on this, we will encourage participants to create their own devices with their own big questions to challenge the rest of the group.

Objective

To inspire the creation of reflective devices for managing data related to technological autonomy.

Time

1:30 h

Moments

  1. Devices on autonomy, an inspiration (30 min)

Arrival at the space and interaction with the example devices. 5 different devices will be installed to allow participants to reflect on their differences… Additionally, there will be a space with inspirational and thought-provoking references.

Introduction to photo taking.

  1. Device 1 - Colored stones: screen consumption
  • Question:
  • How much time do others spend looking at screens?
  • How much time do you spend looking at screens per day?
  • Materials: colored stones, spoon, canvas with a circular drawing, baskets to store the stones.
  1. Device 2 - Decision tree: Sharing and privilege

Ideally, everyone should interact in a group with this device. At the end, each person who follows a path must place a dot.

https://jamboard.google.com/d/1Y34vGSI9O59fpOe5OWD6ck5qWBsgEy-hZfoSQ56dwbI/viewer?f=1&pli=1

  1. Device 3 - Technological privilege ladder

Take a step if your power doesn’t go out frequently.

Take a step forward if you don’t usually have trouble accessing the internet.

Take a step forward if you haven’t felt worried about the amount of gigabytes you consume on the internet.

Take a step forward if you have a postpaid internet plan.

Take a step forward if you have ever bought a cell phone for more than 300 USD.

Take a step forward if you have never had to solve an internet problem on your own.

Take a step forward if you have more computers than the number of people in your house.

Take a step forward if you pay for more than 2 internet services (e.g., Netflix, Spotify, Google Drive, iCloud, Mubi, meditation services, ChatGPT, etc.).

Take a step if you don’t usually spend time in public recreational and cultural spaces.

  • Materials: Masking tape, printout with the script of the questions.
  1. Device 4 - Ribbons: call to autonomous action
  • How much of a “tinkerer” (cacharrero) do you consider yourself?
  • How curious are you about how the devices you use in your daily life work?
  • Materials: Colored ribbons, 3 sheets of poster board.
  1. Device 5 - LEGO data; Awareness-information

If one LEGO brick represents 10 GB, place your assessments in relation to the following questions:

  • How many GB of internet do you think you consume per month?
  • How many GB of internet do you think you need?

One person consumes more than 500 GB per month.

  1. Library of references

We will leave a section where people can go to consult other ways of presenting data.

  1. Presentation, workshop exhibition, and reflection on the data (15 minutes)

We present SOLE and Voltaje, and the tensions that emerge in our projects regarding data management, and finally, we move with the group of participants to reflect on their answers in the 4 devices.

Reflections

a. Device 1 - Colored stones: screen consumption.

The importance of tinkering (cacharrería) for the Latin American context, and how these forms allow for an approach to knowledge and local ways of learning.

b. Device 2 - Decision tree: Sharing and privilege

The paradoxes that distance our beliefs from our practices, and the mechanisms to mobilize them.

c. Device 3 - Privilege ladder: We are positioned from different places and histories, which enables or hinders our journey on Earth.

d. Device 4 - Ribbons: Estimating my internet usage

Real estimates of the cost of each type of consumption and a national comparison of the prices charged.

e. Device 5 - LEGO data; Awareness-information

Changing habits regarding access to online and offline information, new logics of cultural consumption.

General: Reflection, dialogue, and questioning VS extraction.

  1. Designing your own devices (30 minutes)

  • Two truths and a lie regarding technology (10 minutes)

    People will say two truths and a lie regarding technology, and the others will have to guess which one is the lie.

  • Device design (20 minutes)

Design a quick device to challenge, reflect, and dialogue about the use of technology in both your personal life and the organizations where you work. Think of questions that the rest of the participants can answer and that can generate great conversations and new questions on the subject.

  • Materials: Table with possible materials for designing the devices, adhesive tape, newspaper, post-its, markers, colored pencils, colored stones, ribbons, yarn, sheets of paper, adhesive dots, lentils.
  1. Interaction with the devices (15 minutes)
  • Quick exhibition of the created devices and the defined questions.
  • Invitation for participants to interact with these devices throughout the day.
  • We close with a general reflection and a thank you.
    • Resource economy
    • The particularity of a reflective VS extractive question
    • The power of visualization in questioning our habits, beliefs, expectations, etc.
