"How can technology help us weave stories?"

Methodology for weaving stories using technology.

Implementation site: Recharge your SOLE LAB in Boca de Camarones

Implementation date:

2023/11/04

Figure 1. Photograph showing the connections participants made regarding their life experiences in relation to technology.

Purpose: To recognize each other as part of the SOLE Lab project through our life stories in relation to technology.

Device instructions

  1. Tell your life story through what technology has allowed you to do. To do this, select 3 milestones or relevant moments and draw them in a vignette.

  2. How do our stories connect? We will use yarn to connect everyone’s stories.

Device development

  • The experiences narrated below are the result of what each participant said about their life experiences in relation to technology. To avoid misrepresentation, we use ““ as a way to quote what the person actually said. In other parts of the text, the person is paraphrased, trying to remain faithful to what was expressed throughout the session.
  • We use the word milestone to refer to those important events or happenings that are relevant to the person telling them.
  • The order of the names appearing in this systematization is the order in which people connected the stories. For example: Óscar started by telling his three milestones. Subsequently, Lizeth from SOLE Colombia followed, because she connected what she had drawn with one or several of Óscar’s milestones.
  • There were moments in the session where the speaker connected with the stories of several participants who had already spoken.

Óscar [Boca de Camarones]:

  • Milestone 1: “My first TV was the first one in the community.”
  • Milestone 2: “When I gave presentations at school, others did it with a Video Beam and I did it manually; this made me feel inferior. However, the teachers valued my posters much more because of how creative they were.”
  • Milestone 3: “I didn’t touch a computer until I was eleven (11) years old. I locked that computer up and was very afraid of technology.”

Lizeth [SOLE Colombia]:

  • Milestone 1: Her father bought a cell phone that became the community’s phone.
  • Milestone 2: The video game machine at her parents’ store allowed her to have more popularity and friends.
  • Milestone 3: “I filled up on floppy disks of my favorite series and learned many songs.”

Elainer [Boca de Camarones]:

  • Milestone 1: His first contact with computers was while studying.
  • Milestone 2: He worked at a Punto Vive Digital and was able to help many people.
  • Milestone 3: “I found SOLE Colombia and lived the experience of setting up a SOLE LAB in my community.”

Marcelo [Boca de Camarones]:

  • Milestone 1: He helped at the Punto Vive Digital where he worked with his brother. He says that in his childhood he knew nothing about technology, as he hadn’t had contact with those “gadgets.”
  • Milestone 2: He recognizes the moment when the SOLE LAB materials arrived.
  • Milestone 3: His first cell phone. “My first cell phone broke.”

Sebastián [audiovisual production]:

  • Milestone 1: “They gave me my first tape recorder. ‘Let the music play!’” he said.
  • Milestone 2: “They gave me my first guitar. ‘Let’s make some noise!’” he said.
  • Milestone 3: He talked about his first computer and how exciting it was to know everything he could do with it.

Gustavo [Internet Society Foundation]:

  • Milestone 1: Since he was little, he liked making magazines. When he was a kid, he started writing a blog. “This was my first job at a digital newspaper.”
  • Milestone 2: “My first cell phone allowed me to see the world without being afraid of the information I could find.”
  • Milestone 3: “Video calls allow me to connect with my sister and my nephews.”

Ana María [Boca de Camarones]:

  • Milestone 1: “Through the internet, I connect with my friends in Aracataca.”
  • Milestone 2: “From age 9 to 11, I had my first MP3 player.” She says she has always liked listening to music. “I would take over the remote for our first DVD player and wouldn’t lend it to my brothers.”
  • Milestone 3: I met Marcelo (her partner) through social media. “He lived in Santa Marta and we couldn’t see each other,” she said.

Mateo [SOLE Colombia]:

  • Milestone 1: He remembers his first heartbreak email. “I was writing to someone I liked, but who didn’t pay attention to me.”
  • Milestone 2: “My aunt gave me my first analog camera. It had a roll with 20 photos.”
  • Milestone 3: “My first computer broke. It was frustrating for my parents, who had gone into debt for it.”

Nicolas [SOLE Colombia]:

  • Milestone 1: He was calling a radio station asking for tickets to a Soda Stereo concert. “The station answered me, but the dog took the internet cable and the call to the station was cut off.”
  • Milestone 2: He remembers his first video game and Game Boy.
  • Milestone 3: His father taught him how to ride a bike.

Caril [Boca de Camarones]:

  • Milestone 1: “I learned to use a calculator at age 9. I didn’t know how to use it, I was scared, but I learned.”
  • Milestone 2: She learned to use a laptop. “The internet has been a great tool for learning how to use technology,” she said.
  • Milestone 3: “Thanks to SOLE, I’ve gotten into the world of networks and technology. I want to keep learning.”

SanJay [SOLE Colombia]:

  • Milestone 1: “I bought a sampler to make music.” It gave him a lot of happiness because he felt he could make his own music.
  • Milestone 2: “At 16, I won a scholarship to study in Hong Kong and I bought a laptop and the movie Matrix on CD. The movie was divided into two parts; my brother scratched CD 2 trying to put it into the DVD player, but it got damaged and we couldn’t keep watching the movie.” He says it was frustrating, although now it makes him laugh.
  • Milestone 3: “I started making my own music.”

Paola [audiovisual production]:

  • Milestone 1: “They gave me my first camera. It was red. I’m nearsighted, but I learned how to use it. I’ve collected many cameras throughout my life because I like keeping the memory of moments.”
  • Milestone 2: “I remember my first cell phone looked like a brick.”
  • Milestone 3: “The internet arrived at the house. This gave me the possibility to connect with my friends in Uruguay.”

Findings and takeaways

  • In the session, participants connected their stories emotionally and intuitively. There was no sense of moral or material superiority between the stories.
  • Having to connect with another person’s story keeps everyone in a constant state of listening.
  • It is interesting how stories about the first technological device appear—whether it’s a TV, a cell phone, a printer, etc.—and how these devices allow new relationships with other people to be generated.
  • It is interesting how life stories in relation to technology say a lot about who we are and what we have stopped being today. Many stories connect with people’s passions, tastes, or character traits.
  • The milestones are related to happy, pleasant stories, but also to those that generate shame, pain, or nostalgia.
  • The importance of these devices lies not in the story itself, but in the way people connect their stories to one another.
